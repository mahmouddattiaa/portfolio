# Kepler Dev — Market Research and Entry Decision

**Stage 2, Step 1**  
**Research date:** 8 August 2026  
**Planning horizon:** First 90 days, with a one-year agency vision

## Executive decision

Kepler Dev should enter the market as a **founder-led, mobile-first product studio for operational businesses**, not as a general software agency.

The best first buyer is:

> **The founder or owner of a growing GCC service, retail, mobility, or field-operations business whose customer and staff workflows are still fragmented across WhatsApp, spreadsheets, paper, or disconnected tools.**

The first core offer should be:

> **Design and build the first production-ready version of a connected operational product: a focused customer or staff application, a lightweight management dashboard, the backend, integrations, deployment, and launch support.**

This matches Kepler Dev's strongest real work—gLiter's customer/worker/platform ecosystem, QuickChargingPOS, HS VPN, and other mobile and business-system projects—more closely than a generic “MVP agency” claim.

The recommended market-entry sequence is:

1. **Upwork/international:** use as the fastest demand-testing and cash-flow channel.
2. **Saudi/GCC direct outreach:** build as the primary higher-value market and long-term positioning.
3. **Egypt:** use selectively for referrals, relationships, proof, and recruiting—not as the main pricing benchmark.

The brand may still accept startup MVPs, web applications, and dashboards. Those are delivery capabilities; they should not all appear as equal positioning messages.

## 1. Research question

This research was designed to answer five practical questions:

1. Is there current demand for Kepler Dev's capabilities?
2. Which buyer has the strongest combination of demand, budget, accessibility, and portfolio fit?
3. Which services should lead the agency's positioning?
4. What pricing territory is credible for a new founder-led studio?
5. What should be tested before investing meaningful money?

### Evidence used

- Official market reports from Upwork, Saudi government-related sources, ITIDA, and the U.S. International Trade Administration.
- A directional sample of current Upwork jobs covering mobile MVPs, operational systems, AI integration, and full-stack product work.
- Public positioning and pricing from relevant Egyptian, Saudi, and international development agencies.
- Kepler Dev's current skills, available time, financial constraints, and existing project proof.

This is **decision-grade desk research**, not a complete statistical market survey. Its conclusions should be validated through proposals, outreach, and buyer conversations during the next 30 days.

## 2. Market signals

### 2.1 Upwork and international remote demand

Demand for software specialists remains healthy. Upwork's 2026 research says that 77% of business leaders believe AI is increasing their need for specialized fractional talent. Demand for explicitly AI-related skills grew 109% year over year, while core disciplines such as web development remained important. The implication is not that Kepler Dev must become an “AI agency”; it is that buyers increasingly expect normal products and workflows to include useful AI where appropriate. ([Upwork: In-Demand Skills 2026](https://www.upwork.com/research/in-demand-skills-2026))

Upwork's late-2025 hiring reports also show demand for JavaScript, Python, chatbots, and workflow automation. The December report notes that small and medium businesses were hiring for operational needs rather than experimentation alone. ([November 2025 report](https://www.upwork.com/research/monthly-hiring-report-november-2025), [December 2025 report](https://www.upwork.com/research/monthly-hiring-report-december-2025))

Current platform pricing is highly dispersed:

- Upwork cites **$3,000–$8,000** for a basic mobile MVP and **$3,000–$15,000** for a standard app, with mobile developer rates commonly around **$18–$39/hour**. ([Upwork mobile developer guide](https://www.upwork.com/hire/mobile-app-developers/))
- Web development commonly appears around **$15–$50/hour**, while full-stack work is often **$16–$35/hour**. ([Web developer guide](https://www.upwork.com/hire/web-developers/cost/), [full-stack guide](https://www.upwork.com/hire/full-stack-developers/cost/))
- Clutch's broader agency data places most reviewed app and custom software projects between **$10,000 and $49,999**, with common agency rates around **$25–$49/hour**. This represents established-agency territory, not Kepler Dev's required starting price. ([Clutch app pricing](https://clutch.co/directory/mobile-application-developers/pricing), [Clutch software pricing](https://clutch.co/developers/pricing))

The live-job sample shows two different markets living on the same platform:

- Severe low-budget noise: complex systems advertised for tens or hundreds of dollars.
- Qualified projects: approximately **$1,500–$20,000+**, often with milestone-based delivery, paid pilots, and detailed specifications.

Examples include a [$20,000 Next.js production build](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Next-Developer-for-Directory-Site-Build-Full-Spec-Design-Ready-Fixed-Price_~022085548114658259213/), a [$10,000 platform requiring Saudi payment and communication integrations](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Tech-Partner-for-Digital-Platform-Development_~022081105924992265842/), a [$4,000 React Native and AI mobile MVP](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/React-Native-Developer-Team-Needed-Build-Fashion-Mobile-App-MVP_~022080000092694583168/), and a Saudi [$1,500, 15-screen Flutter/React Native MVP](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Flutter-React-Native-Developer-Needed-Screen-Mobile-App-MVP_~022079549102476769030/).

The most attractive buyers repeatedly ask for:

- A senior developer or small product team capable of ownership.
- Architecture and scope recommendations, not only coding.
- Plain-English communication and realistic trade-offs.
- Working demonstrations and milestone visibility.
- Production deployment, app-store release, documentation, and support.
- Experience with payments, messaging, offline sync, dashboards, multiple user roles, and AI integrations.

This language fits Kepler Dev's intended advisory communication style and existing technical range.

### 2.2 Saudi/GCC opportunity

Saudi Arabia offers the strongest direct-market opportunity. ICT spending was expected to reach **$39.6 billion in 2025**, driven by digitization, cloud, AI, smart-city programs, and investment across major sectors. Public-cloud spending was forecast to exceed **$2.7 billion in 2025** and reach **$5 billion in 2027**. The same market guide identifies practical opportunities in asset tracking, fleet management, warehouse optimization, and operational effectiveness—all relevant to mobile-enabled business systems. ([U.S. International Trade Administration: Saudi ICT](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/saudi-arabia-information-and-communications-technology), [Saudi digital economy](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/saudi-arabia-digital-economy-0))

Saudi Arabia had approximately **1.3 million SMEs** by mid-2024, providing a large underlying base of possible operational-software buyers. ([Monsha'at](https://monshaat.gov.sa/en/node/53859))

The opportunity comes with higher expectations:

- Arabic/English user experience may be required.
- Saudi payment methods, WhatsApp/SMS, maps, and local business integrations can influence the purchase decision.
- Saudi personal-data rules and cross-border transfer requirements must be assessed before choosing hosting and data architecture. ([Saudi data-transfer enforcement overview](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/saudi-arabia-ict-cross-border-data-transfer-rules-now-under-enforcement))
- Trust, local understanding, visible proof, and reliable communication matter more than simply offering a low hourly rate.

Kepler Dev should therefore approach GCC clients with business outcomes and delivery confidence—not with “Egyptian developer prices.” Cost efficiency can support the margin, but it should not be the headline.

### 2.3 Egypt's role

Egypt is an increasingly credible base for exporting technology services. ITIDA reported digital offshoring exports reaching **$4.8 billion in 2025**, double the 2022 figure, with more than 240 offshoring companies operating in the country. ([ITIDA offshoring announcement](https://itida.gov.eg/English/PressReleases/Pages/Egypt-Doubles-Digital-Offshoring-Exports-to-4.8B-Dollars.aspx))

ITIDA describes more than 270 delivery centres serving over 100 countries, including clients in Europe, North America, and the GCC. ([ITIDA industry outlook](https://itida.gov.eg/English/Programs/Industry-Outlook/Pages/default.aspx)) This supports Kepler Dev's long-term plan to serve international clients while hiring Egyptian talent.

Egypt should initially play four roles:

1. Source of skilled contractors as revenue grows.
2. Credibility as an established regional technology-delivery location.
3. Selective source of warm referrals and suitable local projects.
4. Future test market for Kepler Labs products.

It should not determine Kepler Dev's international pricing. Local work is worth accepting when it brings strong proof, repeat revenue, a strategic relationship, or a genuinely profitable scope.

## 3. Competitive landscape

The market is crowded with broad claims:

- [Blink22](https://www.blink22.com/) positions around custom software for startups and enterprises, with web, mobile, and emerging technologies.
- [Code95](https://code95.com/services/) offers planning, UX/UI, web, mobile, integrations, testing, launch support, SEO, and broader digital services across Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and MENA.
- [Simublade](https://www.simublade.com/location/mobile-app-development-company-in-saudi-arabia) promotes very rapid, investor-ready startup MVP delivery.
- [Neologix](https://neologix.sa/services/mobile-development/) emphasizes enterprise mobile applications connected to real business processes, ERP/CRM systems, live data, and secure workflows.

The competitive lesson is clear: “web, mobile, AI, cloud and UI/UX” is not a differentiator. Nearly every agency can publish that list.

Kepler Dev's early differentiation should instead combine:

- **Founder-led accountability:** the client works directly with the person responsible for the product and technical decisions.
- **Connected operations:** customer experience, staff workflow, dashboard, backend, and integrations are treated as one product system.
- **Mobile-first depth:** Flutter/React Native, native integrations, offline behaviour, notifications, payments, hardware or POS connections where needed.
- **Scope discipline:** begin with a blueprint and a focused first release rather than promising a giant platform at an unrealistic price.
- **Launch ownership:** include deployment, handover, documentation, and an explicit post-launch option.

This position is specific enough to be memorable while still allowing startup MVP and web-platform work that fits the same capabilities.

### Chosen wedge

**Buyer:** GCC owner/founder with a real operational workflow and a small-to-medium team.  
**Problem:** customers, staff, and managers work through disconnected manual tools.  
**Outcome:** one focused, launch-ready operational product with a mobile interface and management visibility.  
**Proof theme:** Kepler Dev has already built products combining multiple user roles, mobile workflows, dashboards, cloud systems, and real-world integrations.

### Secondary Upwork buyer

A non-technical or product-led founder who:

- Has validated the need or already has users.
- Has designs/specifications or will pay for discovery.
- Needs mobile or full-stack product ownership.
- Values launch, deployment, and support.
- Can fund a tightly scoped first version at **$1,500+**, with preference for **$3,000+** opportunities.

## 5. Recommended service architecture

### Entry offer: Product & Operations Blueprint

A paid, low-risk discovery engagement that produces:

- Business and user-flow clarification.
- First-release scope and exclusions.
- Recommended architecture and integrations.
- Milestones, timeline, budget range, and risk register.
- A proposal for the build phase.

**Initial validation price:** approximately **$300–$750** for international/Upwork clients and **$500–$1,200** for direct GCC clients, adjusted for complexity. These are Kepler Dev launch prices, not permanent rates.

### Core offer: Launch-Ready Product Sprint

A focused build containing one core workflow, usually with:

- One customer-facing or staff-facing mobile/web experience.
- Lightweight admin dashboard.
- Backend, authentication, data model, and essential integrations.
- Deployment, documentation, and launch support.

**Initial target territory:** **$2,500–$6,000** for a tightly scoped first release. Larger multi-app ecosystems must be sold in phases rather than forced into this range.

### Continuity offer: Product Care

Post-launch support covering monitoring, maintenance, bug fixes, releases, and a controlled amount of improvement work.

This creates recurring revenue and gives Kepler Dev a reason to remain involved after launch. Exact retainer tiers should be designed after the first buyer interviews and a realistic review of support workload.

### Applied AI

AI should be presented as an optional product capability:

- Assist users inside an existing workflow.
- Extract or classify information.
- Generate summaries or recommendations.
- Automate repetitive internal steps.

Do not present generic chatbots as the main agency identity. Buyers increasingly want AI, but reliable integration into a useful product is more defensible than the label “AI agency.”

## 6. Upwork qualification rules

### Pursue

- Budget of at least $1,500 for a focused build, or a credible hourly engagement.
- Verified payment history and evidence that the client hires seriously.
- Mobile, dashboard, backend, AI integration, deployment, or operational complexity matching Kepler Dev's proof.
- A clear brief, existing design/specification, or willingness to begin with paid discovery.
- Milestones, demonstrations, technical recommendations, and post-launch support are valued.
- The client asks for a senior developer or small accountable team.

### Avoid

- Full multi-role products below $1,000.
- “Uber/Airbnb/Tinder clone” expectations without scope reduction.
- Complete platforms promised in one or two weeks.
- Unpaid architecture, extensive test tasks, or speculative discovery.
- A vague scope combined with a fixed, non-negotiable price.
- Buyers selecting primarily by the lowest bid.

Kepler Dev should compete on fit and reduced delivery risk, not proposal volume or the cheapest rate.

## 7. First 30-day validation plan

Desk research becomes useful only when prospects respond. Spend little and collect evidence.

### Experiment A — Upwork

- Submit **20 highly selective proposals** over four weeks.
- Split the sample: 10 mobile/product MVPs and 10 operational systems, dashboards, or AI integrations.
- Personalize every proposal around one relevant proof point and one product-risk observation.
- Offer a paid blueprint when the scope is uncertain.

Track: viewed proposals, replies, calls, paid discovery, wins, stated budget, and objection.

### Experiment B — Saudi/GCC conversations

- Identify **30 owner-led businesses** in sectors such as mobility, field services, fuel/energy services, retail loyalty, property/service operations, and specialist booking businesses.
- Seek **10 short problem interviews**, not immediate sales presentations.
- Ask how customer requests arrive, how staff complete work, how management monitors it, and where errors or delays occur.
- After a clear problem appears, offer the blueprint rather than an entire platform quotation.

Track: problem frequency, financial consequence, current workaround, decision-maker, urgency, existing vendor, and acceptable next step.

### Experiment C — Message test

Test two messages without changing the underlying capability:

1. “We build launch-ready mobile MVPs for founders.”
2. “We replace fragmented customer and staff workflows with one mobile-first operational product.”

Success is measured by qualified conversations, not likes or website traffic.

### Decision rule after 30 days

- If operations messaging produces more qualified GCC calls, make it the homepage lead and build an operations-focused case study.
- If founder MVP proposals produce faster paid work, keep them as the near-term sales engine while GCC outreach matures.
- If neither generates meaningful replies, revise the proof and message before reducing prices.

## 8. Financial discipline

This research does not justify spending the $2,000 savings reserve.

For the first month:

- Use the existing domain and portfolio infrastructure.
- Keep optional spend below roughly **$100–$200** for Upwork connects, essential outreach tools, or small operating costs.
- Do not pay for broad ads, a large rebrand, an office, permanent staff, or expensive software subscriptions.
- Hire contractors only after a signed scope and client deposit make the engagement affordable.
- Aim to collect **40–50% upfront** on direct fixed-price work, followed by milestone payments.

The first $2,000/month should come from one focused build plus discovery/support work, or two smaller engagements—not a high volume of cheap websites.

## 9. Working market position

### One-sentence positioning

> **Kepler Dev designs and builds mobile-first products and operational platforms for founders and growing GCC businesses—from scope and architecture through launch and support.**

### Sharper GCC campaign message

> **We turn disconnected customer, staff, and management workflows into one focused mobile product and dashboard.**

### Upwork profile direction

> **Senior Flutter/React Native and full-stack product developer for launch-ready MVPs, operational apps, dashboards, integrations, and deployment.**

The agency can grow into multiple teams and Kepler Labs later. For the first 90 days, the market should experience Kepler Dev as a small, senior, accountable studio with a narrow promise and wider execution capability.

## Final conclusion

There is enough market demand to proceed, and Kepler Dev's capabilities are commercially relevant. The risk is not a lack of services to sell; it is entering the market with a message so broad that buyers cannot distinguish the agency.

The correct starting model is an **honest founder-led boutique with agency-level delivery discipline**. Do not fake a large team. Present the capacity to bring in vetted specialists when scope requires it, while keeping Mahmoud visibly accountable for discovery, architecture, and delivery.

The next step is **Stage 2, Step 2: offer design and positioning**—turning this market decision into one clear primary offer, scope boundaries, pricing logic, buyer language, and a qualification process.
