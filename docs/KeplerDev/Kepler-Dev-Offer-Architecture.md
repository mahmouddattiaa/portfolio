# Kepler Dev — Offer Architecture

**Stage 2, Workstream C**  
**Version:** 0.1 — Working commercial model  
**Date:** 8 August 2026

## 1. Purpose

This document turns Kepler Dev's capabilities into clearly purchasable services. It defines what each offer accomplishes, where its boundaries are, how it is priced, and how a client moves from one engagement to the next.

The commercial model must satisfy five constraints:

1. Mahmoud initially has only 20–30 hours per week for the agency.
2. Kepler Dev starts founder-led and uses contractors only when funded by signed work.
3. The agency must not absorb unlimited revisions or undefined product scope.
4. The first revenue goal is $2,000 per month without exhausting the $2,000 savings reserve.
5. The offers must support the long-term transition into multiple teams and Kepler Labs products.

---

## 2. Offer strategy

### Flagship position

> **Kepler Dev turns fragmented customer, staff, and management workflows into one focused mobile-first operational product.**

### Primary buyer

The founder or owner of a growing GCC service, retail, mobility, fuel/energy-service, booking, field-operation, or loyalty business who currently depends on WhatsApp, spreadsheets, paper, manual approvals, or disconnected software.

### Secondary buyer

A nontechnical or product-led international founder who has a validated problem, a realistic first-release scope, and the budget to build and launch it properly.

### Offer ladder

| Offer | Purpose | Typical duration | Working starting range | Primary channel |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Kepler Product Blueprint | Turn an uncertain idea/problem into a buildable plan | 5–10 business days | $350–$1,200 | All channels |
| Kepler Launch Sprint | Ship one focused, production-ready MVP | 6–10 weeks | $3,000–$8,000 | Upwork/international |
| Kepler Operations Platform | Digitize one core business workflow across users and management | 8–16+ weeks, phased | $6,000–$15,000+ for Phase 1 | GCC direct |
| Kepler Product Care | Protect and improve a launched product | Monthly | $400–$1,500+ per month | Existing clients |

These are working launch ranges, not promises. Final pricing depends on discovery, interfaces, integrations, risk, delivery speed, and contractor requirements.

# Offer 1 — Kepler Product Blueprint

## 3. Intended buyer

- A founder with a product idea but no reliable technical scope.
- A business owner who knows an operation is inefficient but does not know what software should be built.
- A buyer requesting a fixed build price before the requirements are clear.
- A client with an existing system who needs an architecture, modernization, or AI-integration plan.

## 4. Starting problem

The buyer has a goal, feature list, or operational pain, but no shared definition of the first release. Quoting the full build immediately would create a high risk of underpricing, delay, rework, and disagreement.

## 5. Promised outcome

> A decision-ready, technically credible plan that defines what to build first, what to postpone, how the system should work, what it will cost, and how delivery will be controlled.

The promise is clarity and risk reduction—not guaranteed market success.

## 6. Deliverables

The standard Blueprint includes:

- One 60–90 minute discovery workshop.
- Business goal, buyer, users, and triggering problem summary.
- Current-workflow or proposed-user-flow map.
- First-release scope with explicit exclusions.
- Prioritized feature list: must-have, later, and rejected-for-now.
- Recommended system architecture and technology approach.
- Interfaces, integrations, data, deployment, and compliance considerations.
- Delivery milestones and acceptance checkpoints.
- Estimated timeline and investment range.
- Key assumptions, dependencies, and risks.
- Build proposal for the next phase.

Optional additions:

- Clickable prototype.
- Existing-code audit.
- Data/privacy review.
- Integration feasibility spike.
- Proof-of-concept for a technically uncertain feature.

## 7. Timeline

- Standard: 5–10 business days.
- Complex audit or prototype: separately scoped.

## 8. Pricing and payment

- International/Upwork starting range: **$350–$900**.
- Direct GCC starting range: **$500–$1,200**.
- 100% paid before scheduling.
- If a feature spike, prototype, or substantial code audit is required, it is quoted separately.

The Blueprint should normally require no more than 8–20 founder hours. If the work is larger, it is not the standard Blueprint.

## 9. Client responsibilities

- Provide a decision-maker for the workshop.
- Share existing documents, designs, systems, and known constraints.
- Answer questions and review the draft within two business days.
- Disclose deadlines, budget limits, compliance requirements, and third-party dependencies.

## 10. Revision and acceptance

- One consolidated revision round is included.
- The engagement is accepted when all listed documents are delivered and the consolidated revision is applied.
- New workflows, user groups, product directions, or prototypes are change requests.

## 11. Exclusions

- Production application development.
- Full UI design system.
- Legal, financial, security-certification, or regulatory advice.
- Unlimited stakeholder workshops.
- Guaranteed fixed build price where material unknowns remain.

## 12. Continuation path

- Kepler Launch Sprint for startup products.
- Kepler Operations Platform for operational businesses.
- A handover package if the client chooses another development team.

The client owns the paid Blueprint whether or not Kepler Dev performs the build.

# Offer 2 — Kepler Launch Sprint

## 13. Intended buyer

An international or GCC founder who has validated the problem, can identify the first target user, and needs a serious first product—not a collection of every possible feature.

## 14. Starting problem

The buyer needs a usable product released to test users or early customers, but lacks a senior technical partner who can control scope, build the system, deploy it, and communicate trade-offs.

## 15. Promised outcome

> A focused, production-ready first release that supports one core user journey, can be demonstrated to real users, and has a clear path for iteration.

“Production-ready” means the agreed release can be deployed and operated. It does not mean enterprise scale, every requested feature, or zero future maintenance.

## 16. Standard scope

A standard Launch Sprint normally includes:

- One primary product surface: mobile application or web application.
- One core user type and one core journey.
- Authentication and essential account management.
- Backend, database, and required APIs.
- Lightweight administration interface.
- Up to two ordinary third-party integrations.
- Basic analytics, logging, and error monitoring.
- Responsive UI implementation from agreed designs.
- Test environment and production deployment.
- One app-store submission cycle when mobile publishing is included.
- Handover documentation and one training session.
- Fourteen-day defect warranty after acceptance.

## 17. Typical exclusions

- Multiple independent mobile apps.
- Marketplace complexity with several user types and settlement logic.
- Advanced realtime chat, video, social feed, or recommendation engine.
- Offline-first synchronization unless explicitly scoped.
- Custom AI model training.
- Complex hardware, POS, ERP, banking, or government integrations.
- Full branding, marketing website, content creation, or launch marketing.
- Data migration from poorly documented systems.
- Unlimited device support or repeated store resubmissions caused by client policy/content issues.
- Features not written into the accepted Blueprint or statement of work.

Excluded items may be purchased as later phases.

## 18. Timeline

- Focused release: 6–10 weeks.
- Delivery is divided into visible milestones.
- A project requiring more than approximately 180–220 delivery hours should be restructured into phases or moved to the Operations Platform offer.

## 19. Pricing and payment

- Working range: **$3,000–$8,000**.
- Minimum credible Upwork exception: **$2,500**, only for a narrow, low-risk scope with no hidden platform complexity.
- Projects under $5,000: 50% start, 30% functional milestone, 20% before production handover.
- Projects from $5,000: 40% start, 30% first functional milestone, 20% release candidate, 10% before production handover.
- Marketplace fees, cloud usage, domains, paid APIs, store accounts, and third-party licenses are client expenses.

## 20. Client responsibilities

- One named decision-maker.
- Approved scope and designs before corresponding implementation begins.
- Content, brand assets, legal pages, and third-party accounts.
- Feedback within two business days.
- Test users and business validation.
- App-store/legal declarations that only the business owner can make.

Client-caused delays move the schedule; they do not create free rush work later.

## 21. Revision policy

- Two review cycles per visual milestone.
- Defects against written acceptance criteria are fixed without additional charge.
- Preference changes, newly requested behavior, and additional states are scope changes.
- Change requests receive a written price and schedule effect before work begins.

## 22. Completion and acceptance

A milestone is accepted when:

- The agreed acceptance criteria pass in the designated test environment.
- The client has five business days to submit one consolidated defect list.
- No response after the review period constitutes milestone acceptance, provided the milestone was demonstrably delivered.

Final handover occurs after full payment.

## 23. Continuation path

- A second build phase based on user feedback.
- Kepler Product Care.
- New integrations or an operations layer.

# Offer 3 — Kepler Operations Platform

## 24. Intended buyer

A GCC business owner or operations leader with a repeated workflow involving customers, employees, field workers, branches, transactions, bookings, loyalty, fulfilment, assets, or management approvals.

## 25. Starting problem

The business has operational friction such as:

- Requests scattered across calls and WhatsApp.
- Manual staff assignment and status tracking.
- No shared customer or transaction history.
- Poor visibility across branches or field teams.
- Loyalty, payments, inventory, or service records living in separate tools.
- Repeated data entry and preventable mistakes.

## 26. Promised outcome

> One connected operational product that digitizes a valuable workflow, gives each user the information and actions they need, and gives management reliable visibility.

Kepler Dev should sell the operational improvement—not “three apps and a dashboard.” Technology is the implementation of the business outcome.

## 27. Phase structure

### Phase 0 — Blueprint

Mandatory unless the client already has an adequate specification.

### Phase 1 — Operational Core

Usually includes:

- One priority workflow from request to completion.
- One main mobile or web interface for customers or staff.
- Role-based backend and data model.
- Management dashboard for essential visibility and control.
- Notifications and one or two necessary integrations.
- Deployment, monitoring, documentation, and training.

### Later phases

May add:

- A second dedicated application.
- Branch and permission depth.
- Payments, loyalty, wallets, or invoicing.
- POS, hardware, maps, ERP, CRM, or government integrations.
- Offline synchronization.
- Advanced analytics and automation.
- Applied AI features.

This phased model prevents a full gLiter-sized ecosystem from being sold as one cheap MVP.

## 28. Timeline

- Phase 0: 1–2 weeks.
- Phase 1: commonly 8–16 weeks.
- Later phases are planned after Phase 1 or priced under a signed roadmap.

## 29. Pricing and payment

- Phase 0: **$500–$1,200+**.
- Phase 1 working range: **$6,000–$15,000+**.
- Projects exceeding Kepler Dev's solo capacity require a funded contractor plan and may have a higher price.
- Standard payment schedule: 40% start, 25% architecture/core milestone, 25% release candidate, 10% before production handover.
- For long phases, smaller paid biweekly milestones may replace the percentage schedule.

No contractor should be committed before the client's corresponding payment has cleared.

## 30. Client responsibilities

In addition to the Launch Sprint responsibilities, the operations client must:

- Provide access to real operational users.
- Confirm current business rules and exception cases.
- Supply integration documentation and vendor contacts.
- Own internal process changes and staff adoption.
- Nominate an operational product owner.
- Review data, privacy, hosting, and compliance obligations with qualified advisers where necessary.

## 31. Boundaries

- Kepler Dev builds the agreed software; it does not guarantee staff adoption, revenue growth, funding, or regulatory approval.
- Integrations are priced only after documentation and access are reviewed.
- Migration quality depends on source-data quality.
- Hardware or POS work requires a technical feasibility step.
- Arabic and English support, RTL design, and localization depth must be explicitly listed.
- Support after the warranty period requires Product Care or a new statement of work.

## 32. Acceptance

Each workflow is tested against scenario-based acceptance criteria agreed before development. Examples include:

- A customer can create a valid request.
- An authorized worker can accept and complete it.
- Management can see the correct status and audit history.
- Required notifications and integrations execute under defined conditions.

## 33. Continuation path

- Later roadmap phases.
- Product Care.
- Analytics, automation, and AI improvements.
- New products for other branches, regions, or business units.

# Offer 4 — Kepler Product Care

## 34. Intended buyer

A client with a product that Kepler Dev has launched or formally audited and accepted into support.

## 35. Promised outcome

> A maintained, monitored product with a reliable path for fixes, releases, and controlled improvements.

## 36. Support categories

### Care Essential — starting at $400/month

- Monitoring review.
- Dependency and security maintenance.
- Defect triage.
- Up to approximately 6 hours of planned technical work.
- Monthly status summary.
- Standard response during Egyptian business days.

### Care Growth — starting at $800/month

- Everything in Essential.
- Up to approximately 14 hours of planned work.
- One scheduled release window each month.
- Small product improvements.
- Monthly product and technical review.

### Care Partner — starting at $1,500/month

- Capacity and service level defined for the product.
- Priority planning and response.
- Regular releases and roadmap participation.
- Contractor capacity may be reserved when funded by the retainer.

Hours are internal capacity controls, not a promise that every request fits. The client purchases access, continuity, and defined service—not unlimited development.

## 37. Terms and boundaries

- Paid monthly in advance.
- Initial three-month commitment is preferred.
- Unused capacity does not roll over unless the agreement says otherwise.
- Emergency, weekend, and public-holiday work is not included by default.
- Major features receive a separate estimate.
- Cloud, API, license, and vendor fees remain client expenses.
- Response time is not the same as resolution time.
- Products built by another team require a paid audit and onboarding period.

---

## 38. Pricing framework

Kepler Dev should never quote only by counting screens. Price must reflect delivery effort, uncertainty, responsibility, and business risk.

### Internal calculation

Use this model before presenting a fixed price:

> **Project price = founder delivery value + contractor cost allowance + operating expenses + risk reserve**

Working rules:

- Founder internal sell-rate floor: **$30/hour** for international work.
- Direct GCC planning range: **$40–$55/hour equivalent**, even when the proposal is fixed price.
- Unknown legacy code, urgent rescue work, and undefined research should be hourly or preceded by a Blueprint.
- Contractor cost should normally be no more than 50–60% of the revenue attributed to that contractor's work.
- Add a 10–20% risk reserve for fixed-price delivery, depending on uncertainty.
- Add platform fees and currency/transfer costs before checking margin.
- Never reduce price without reducing scope, risk, speed, or included service.

### Complexity drivers

Increase the estimate for:

- Each independent user application or major product surface.
- Each materially different user role and permission model.
- Realtime behavior, offline sync, geolocation, background services, or native device access.
- Payments, financial records, loyalty balances, or audit requirements.
- External systems with incomplete documentation.
- Migration of existing data.
- Arabic/English content and full RTL QA.
- Custom UI/UX work.
- App-store publishing and policy risk.
- Compressed deadlines.
- Sensitive personal or regulated data.

### Discount rules

Permitted discounts must purchase something valuable for Kepler Dev:

- Reduced scope.
- Flexible delivery schedule.
- Upfront payment.
- Reusable public case study and testimonial rights.
- Multi-phase commitment with funded milestones.

Never discount solely because the buyer says another developer is cheaper.

## 39. Standard commercial rules

### Deposits and work start

- Work begins only after the agreement is signed and the first payment clears.
- Discovery is paid in full upfront.
- Build deposits reserve capacity and are not refundable after work begins, subject to the signed contract and applicable law.

### Intellectual property

- Client-specific deliverables transfer after full payment.
- Kepler Dev retains pre-existing tools, reusable utilities, general know-how, and open-source components under their respective licenses.
- Portfolio and case-study rights must be agreed explicitly; confidentiality is respected.

### Warranty

- Launch Sprint: 14-day defect warranty.
- Operations Platform: normally 30 days for the accepted phase.
- A defect is behavior that fails the written acceptance criteria; a new preference or requirement is not a defect.

### Change control

Every material change records:

1. Requested change.
2. Reason and affected requirement.
3. Price effect.
4. timeline effect.
5. New acceptance criteria.
6. Written client approval.

No material change is implemented from an informal chat message alone.

### Pause and cancellation

- The agreement must define payment for completed work and committed costs.
- Client inactivity beyond an agreed period may pause the schedule and require rescheduling.
- Source code and completed paid deliverables are handed over according to the contract; unfinished or unpaid milestones are not treated as accepted deliverables.

## 40. Qualification gate

Before proposing a build, Kepler Dev must be able to answer:

1. Who is the decision-maker?
2. What business or user problem triggers the project?
3. Why is it important now?
4. Who will use the first release?
5. What is the single core workflow?
6. Is the client willing to reduce scope or pay for discovery?
7. Does the budget match the requested responsibility?
8. Are integrations, compliance, content, and account ownership understood?
9. Can the project fit available founder and contractor capacity?
10. Is there a credible payment schedule?

If these questions cannot be answered, sell the Blueprint or decline the build.

## 41. First revenue configuration

The most realistic routes to the first $2,000 month are:

### Route A

- One $3,000–$4,000 Launch Sprint with milestone revenue recognized across delivery.
- One $350–$750 Blueprint.

### Route B

- Two $1,000–$1,500 tightly scoped technical phases, only when they do not pretend to be full products.
- One small support arrangement.

### Route C

- One paid GCC Blueprint.
- One Phase 1 deposit for an Operations Platform.

The goal is not to collect ten cheap websites. The goal is to prove that Kepler Dev can sell accountable product ownership.

## 42. Decisions still requiring validation

This offer model is commercially coherent, but the following must be tested during sales conversations:

- Whether the word “operations” resonates with GCC buyers or needs sector-specific language.
- Whether direct buyers prefer a fixed Phase 1 price or paid biweekly milestones.
- How much UI/UX design Kepler Dev can confidently include without a design contractor.
- Which response times are sustainable for Product Care.
- Whether Arabic-first collateral materially improves response rates.
- The smallest Phase 1 that GCC buyers consider valuable.

These are validation questions, not reasons to delay outreach.
