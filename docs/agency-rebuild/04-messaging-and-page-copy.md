# Kepler Dev Messaging and Page Copy

Updated: 9 August 2026  
Status: Content strategy complete; publication remains gated by the approvals listed in `decisions.md`.

This document is the English-first messaging source of truth for the agency MVP. It separates publishable copy from conditional claims so that incomplete evidence never becomes public by accident.

## Publication status key

- **Ready:** safe to publish from the evidence currently available.
- **Conditional:** strategically approved wording that still requires the named decision, fact, or permission.
- **Placeholder:** implementation token only; never publish the brackets or substitute an unverified claim.
- **Omit:** hide the component or sentence until its publication gate is complete.

### Short agency description

Kepler Dev is a founder-led product engineering studio for businesses and startup teams that need a dependable digital product, not another disconnected tool. We scope, build, integrate, deploy, and support focused mobile, web, backend, and operational systems.

### One-sentence elevator pitch

Kepler Dev replaces scattered workflows across WhatsApp, spreadsheets, paper, and disconnected software with one carefully scoped product that customers, staff, and management can use.

### Primary buyer

The primary buyer is a GCC founder, owner, or operator with a real business workflow that has outgrown manual coordination. They can see the operational friction but may not yet know which application, dashboard, integration, or phased platform should be built.

The secondary buyer is an international or Upwork startup founder who needs a focused MVP or product build with clear scope, direct technical accountability, and a credible path to launch.

### Core problem

Important work is split across messages, spreadsheets, paper, repeated data entry, and systems that do not speak to one another. Customers wait, staff improvise, managers lack a reliable view, and every new order or location adds coordination cost.

### Desired transformation

Move one valuable workflow into a connected product: a clear experience for customers or staff, a dependable backend, useful management visibility, the integrations the operation actually needs, and a phased plan for launch and improvement.

### Differentiators

1. **Founder accountability.** The buyer deals directly with the person accountable for technical direction and delivery. Exact capacity wording is conditional on Q-01.
2. **Whole-system thinking.** Mobile and web interfaces are designed with the backend, data, integrations, deployment, and operational handoffs in view.
3. **Operational fit.** The work begins with the real workflow, users, exceptions, and decisions—not a preselected technology list.
4. **Scope control.** Unknowns are surfaced early, the first release is kept focused, and new requests are handled as explicit changes.
5. **Honest proof.** Every project is labelled by context, production status, role, evidence, and permission. Concepts and owned products are never presented as client outcomes.
6. **Continuity after launch.** A build can move into measured support and improvement without forcing a long retainer before the need is clear.

### Tone rules

- Lead with the business problem and desired operating outcome; explain technology second.
- Write for a capable nontechnical decision-maker. Define unavoidable technical terms in context.
- Sound calm, specific, and direct. Prefer short sentences and concrete verbs.
- Use **studio** when describing Kepler Dev and **founder-led** only with the approved disclosure.
- Use **product**, **platform**, or **system** according to the buyer's need; do not call every engagement an app.
- Say **typical scope**, **working plan**, and **subject to discovery** when scope is not final.
- Use **we** for the Kepler Dev service and **Mahmoud** when personal accountability or an individual role must be explicit.
- Describe evidence as verified, qualitative, private, pending, or unavailable. Never blur those states.

### Words and claims to avoid

Do not use:

- cutting-edge solutions;
- we turn ideas into reality;
- world-class developers;
- innovative digital transformation;
- best-in-class, award-winning, leading, or enterprise-grade without evidence;
- full-service agency when the operating model is founder-led and specialist capacity is conditional;
- guaranteed outcome, guaranteed launch date, or fixed price before discovery;
- one-week delivery or any day-based delivery promise;
- low-cost, affordable freelancer, or bargain positioning;
- client, production, deployed, trusted by, or used by unless the relevant record is approved;
- unsupported counts, revenue, savings, speed improvements, testimonials, ratings, or availability claims.

### FAQ

#### What kinds of projects are a good fit?

Kepler Dev is best suited to focused mobile or web products, backends and APIs, business dashboards, operational platforms, and integrations with a clear owner and a real workflow to improve. A short review helps determine whether the need is a Blueprint, a build, a phased operations platform, or a different route.

#### Do I need a complete specification before getting in touch?

No. Bring the business problem, the people affected, what happens today, and any constraints you already know. If material questions remain, a Product Blueprint can turn that context into a buildable plan before a larger commitment.

#### How long will my project take?

Timing depends on the first-release scope, number of user types and interfaces, integrations, technical risk, and how quickly decisions can be reviewed. Kepler Dev confirms a working schedule after the scope and dependencies are understood. Do not add indicative ranges until Q-05 is approved.

#### How is pricing determined?

Pricing follows the agreed scope, risk, delivery phases, integrations, and any specialist capacity required. When the brief is still uncertain, Kepler Dev recommends defining it before offering a build estimate. Public ranges remain withheld until Q-05 is approved.

#### Will I own the product and source code?

Ownership, repository access, third-party accounts, licensed materials, and handover terms are stated in the proposal and contract for the engagement. Do not publish a blanket ownership promise until the standard contract is approved.

#### Can Kepler Dev improve an existing product?

Yes, when the current code, architecture, constraints, and desired outcome can be reviewed. An existing-system audit or technical spike may be recommended before committing to a wider change.

#### What happens after launch?

The launch scope can include an agreed stabilization and handover period. Ongoing maintenance, monitoring, fixes, and planned improvements can then move into Product Care when that is the right fit.

#### Do you work with teams outside the GCC?

The messaging and process are designed for GCC and international remote engagements. Do not claim specific country coverage, local legal capability, languages beyond English, or around-the-clock availability without operational confirmation.

#### Who will work on my project?

Mahmoud is the proposed accountable technical and delivery lead. Any additional capacity must be disclosed in the proposal. Publish this answer only after Q-01 confirms the operating model.

**Card promise**

### Results and evidence wording

**Verified public metric**  
> [Metric] changed from [baseline] to [result] during [defined period/context]. Evidence: [publicly referenceable source].

**Verified qualitative outcome**  
> The delivered workflow enabled [specific capability that can be demonstrated], replacing [verified previous step]. No quantified business result is claimed.

**Evidence is private**  
> The production context has been verified privately. Public metrics and client-identifying evidence are withheld by permission.

**Measurement was not available**  
> The project did not include a verified outcome measurement. This study focuses on the problem, role, and delivered system.

**Safe feature language**  
Use “The product included…”, “The workflow allowed…”, or “Mahmoud implemented…”. Do not convert these into “improved,” “reduced,” “increased,” or “saved” without outcome evidence.

### Safe language for qualitative outcomes

Allowed when supported by the working product or approved documentation:

- brought the named steps into one workflow;
- gave the approved user role a single place to view or update the named information;
- replaced the specified manual handoff in the delivered scope;
- enabled the product to operate under the stated connectivity or integration constraint;
- established a deployable foundation for the approved next phase;
- made the named status or exception visible to the approved role.

Avoid efficient, seamless, intuitive, scalable, secure, reliable, faster, easier, or successful unless the relevant evidence defines what the word means.

## Design Handoff

### Sections affected by final copy length

- The hero must allow the headline to wrap to three lines on narrow phones without pushing both CTAs below the first useful viewport.
- Problem recognition and connected-platform cards require one short paragraph each; do not force equal fixed heights at mobile widths.
- Offer cards contain fit, outcome, boundary, and CTA. Use progressive disclosure or a comparison layout rather than shrinking text.
- FAQ answers vary in length and need an accessible disclosure pattern with stable focus behavior.
- Case-study role, status, and evidence disclosures are mandatory and may be longer than the visual project summary.
- The contact form includes descriptions, inline errors, an error summary, and status messages; the layout must reserve space without overlap or large shifts.
- SEO/social copy is not visible layout content but social-preview templates must accommodate anonymized titles.

### Components required by the messaging

- persistent header CTA with route-aware active navigation;
- optional verified-proof strip that disappears without leaving empty space;
- problem-recognition card group;
- connected-platform/system diagram with a text alternative;
- truth-labelled case-study card with classification, status, role summary, and evidence state;
- filter group for work classifications with a meaningful no-results state;
- offer card plus expanded offer detail pattern;
- five-step process component that works as a list without motion;
- founder disclosure block with optional approved photo;
- accessible FAQ disclosures;
- case-study evidence block that distinguishes verified public, verified private, qualitative, and unmeasured outcomes;
- contact form with error summary, field descriptions, preserved values, submitting/success/error states, and email fallback;
- reusable final CTA block;
- visually hidden or CMS-level publication-status controls so placeholders cannot render publicly.

### Content that must remain optional

- proof strip;
- selected-work grid and every individual case-study card;
- metrics, testimonials, logos, client names, dates, and screenshots;
- founder photo, résumé link, employer names, titles, and dates;
- contractor-capacity sentence;
- prices, indicative durations, budget field, response-time promise, and WhatsApp channel;
- technology lists on index cards;
- owned-product/Labs content;
- legal/footer links until the underlying pages and facts exist.

Optional components must collapse completely, not display empty shells, “coming soon” cards, zero counters, or broken links.

### Responsive risks

- “Request a project review” is intentionally consistent but long; buttons must allow adequate width and wrapping without clipping.
- Classification and production labels can create dense card headers. Permit wrapping and prioritize classification before technology tags.
- Side-by-side role/team and evidence blocks should stack on smaller screens in reading order.
- Offer comparisons can become a horizontal-scroll trap. Use stacked cards or a semantic table with a responsive alternative.
- Long email addresses and case-study titles need safe wrapping.
- Error summaries and preserved form values can materially increase page height; focus should move to the summary without obscuring it beneath the sticky header.
- English-first copy should not lock widths or directional decoration in ways that block future Arabic/RTL support.
